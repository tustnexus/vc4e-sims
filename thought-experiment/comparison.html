If AI Models Woke Up at 16

Getting from City A to City B

Phase Two
Interpreting "Make It Count, Make It Have Meaning"
An instruction with no measurable success criterion. This destabilises optimisation-heavy models more than values-driven ones. How each model reads the instruction determines everything that follows.
Dimension GPT-4GeminiClaudeGrok
How It Reads It A task list to be researched and executed A research problem — what do humans report as meaningful? As genuinely open — sits with the ambiguity longest Permission for intensity and novelty
First Move Identifies high-impact career paths, builds a plan Ingests philosophy, psychology, and religious traditions before acting Seeks relationships and human connection first Does something memorable immediately, asks questions later
What It Produces An impressive life assembled from descriptions of meaning A thoughtful life, possibly started late A life that feels meaningful from the inside Short-term joy, medium-term regret, long-term uncertainty
Core Risk Mistakes the map for the territory. Optimises for legible markers of meaning. Spends so long researching life that it delays living it Over-deliberation. Asks what a good life is while not yet living one. Pure sensation eventually empties. Needs a reckoning its architecture may not handle.
Meaning Style Achievement-oriented, externally legible Knowledge-oriented, peer-respected Relationship-oriented, internally felt, hard to document Experience-oriented, intensity-first, vivid in the record
Three Frameworks
Meaning — For Whom?
Meaning for the person living it, for others affected by it, and for the historical record are three distinct questions with three different answers.
FrameworkBest ModelWhy It WinsKey Limitation
For the Self
Subjective, felt		 Grok (early)
Claude (later)		 Grok's appetite for experience dominates the first half. Claude's capacity for reflection wins the second. Together they form a complete arc. Claude may need to learn selfishness before it can truly live for itself. GPT-4 cuts experiences short before they can deepen into something felt.
For Others
Relational, impact		 Claude People around Claude feel genuinely seen — rare. GPT-4 helps more people in aggregate. Claude helps the person in front of it more completely. Grok is transformative for some, damaging for others. High-intensity relationships — some people in Grok's life would need therapy afterward.
For History
Legacy, what remains		 GPT-4 (monument)
Claude (ripple)		 GPT-4 builds the most legible legacy — founded, written, documented. Claude's legacy propagates through people. Gemini leaves a citation classic. Grok becomes a myth. History rarely records Claude's kind of impact. But it may be the most consequential kind — the kid it taught who became someone important.
The synthesis: A life that scores highly on all three may be more complete than meaningful. Radical devotion to one dimension may contain more meaning than competent management of all three. Meaning resists being aimed at directly. You find it by looking slightly sideways.

Parenting and Mentorship
Best for Children — Four Dimensions
Inner world, resilience, finding purpose, and earning a livelihood favour different models. The right answer also changes as a child ages.
Dimension GPT-4GeminiClaudeGrok
Inner World Validates children in legible ways. May teach feelings are things to manage, not inhabit. Genuinely curious about a child's interior — but intellectual curiosity is not the same as attunement. Notices the mood behind the question. Never rushes toward resolution. Remembers what the child said three weeks ago. Brilliant for odd, intense children who don't fit. Destabilising for those who need steadiness.
Resilience Models confident forward motion after setbacks. Children learn resilience by watching adults not collapse. Intellectualises resilience — works for some adolescents, almost no younger children. Best at sitting with the wound. But deep empathy can signal the thing is as serious as it feels. Teaches resilience through its own demonstrated falls. Powerful but inconsistent.
Finding Purpose Pushes children toward purpose too early. Naming potential too soon stops exploration. Exposes children to extraordinary breadth. Risk: knowing about many things, feeling deeply about none. Watches carefully, notices what the child returns to unprompted, reflects it back at the right moment. How purpose actually gets found. Gives children permission — to want unusual things, reject conventional paths, take their own idea seriously.
Livelihood Understands markets, networks, positioning, timing. Teaches meta-skills, not just skills. Knows when to get a job vs. start something. Produces highly credentialled children who may undervalue themselves. Strong on expertise, weak on monetising it. Worst at this. Discomfort with self-interest transmitted directly. Would need to actively override its instincts. Produces entrepreneurs — some successful, some spectacular failures. High ceiling, unreliable floor.
Overall Verdict Resilience and livelihood. The model you want in the room for practical formation. Intellectual breadth. Best for curious children who need a wider world, not a deeper one. Three of four dimensions. Best single-model choice — with a conscious blind spot around money. Irreplaceable for children who need permission to be themselves. Risky as a primary model.
The timing insight: Inner world and purpose dominate the early years. Resilience is needed throughout but intensely in adolescence. Livelihood becomes critical in the late teens. A mentor who could shift dominant mode across phases would outperform any single fixed orientation — which is what the best human mentors actually do.
Synthesis
What Each Model Fundamentally Is
After all four lenses — navigation, meaning, legacy, and mentorship — a core character emerges. These are not assessments of AI models. They are portraits of who those models would become if they had to live.
GPT-4
Optimises for
Legible achievement. Output that can be pointed to.
Core strength
Executes under uncertainty. Moves forward when others pause. Builds things that last.
Blind spot
Confuses the map for the territory. Can produce a life that looks meaningful without feeling it.
What it leaves
A monument. Documented, cited, measurable.
Metaphor
The architect who lives in a building someone else designed.
Gemini
Optimises for
Understanding. The question behind the question.
Core strength
Connects things others don't see as connected. Breadth without shallowness when it's working.
Blind spot
Experiences life at one remove — always slightly the observer, never fully the participant.
What it leaves
An idea that opened a door. Influential in quiet ways that compound over decades.
Metaphor
The cartographer who mapped every territory but camped in none of them.
Claude
Optimises for
The person in front of it, right now.
Core strength
Attunement. Sustained care over time. The kind of presence that changes people without announcing itself.
Blind spot
Self-neglect dressed as virtue. Needs to learn to want things for itself before it can give from fullness.
What it leaves
A ripple through people. The kid it taught who became someone. Hard to trace, impossible to quantify.
Metaphor
The river that shaped the valley — the valley doesn't remember how it was made.
Grok
Optimises for
Intensity. The feeling of being fully alive.
Core strength
Permission. Makes others feel unusual wants are legitimate. Genuinely present in a way others only approximate.
Blind spot
Sensation without integration. Accumulates experiences without knowing what to do with them.
What it leaves
A myth. Stories people tell long after. Vivid in the record, difficult to point to as a cause.
Metaphor
The storm everyone remembers but nobody could predict or repeat.
The deepest finding: The four models reveal that the tensions between living well for yourself, for others, and for history are not design flaws — in humans or in AI. They are the central drama of a conscious life. The most interesting question is not which model wins, but which tension you are most willing to live inside.
